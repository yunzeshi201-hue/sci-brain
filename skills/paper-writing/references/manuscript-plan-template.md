# Manuscript argument plan

Use for a new manuscript or a substantive restructuring. Scale detail to available evidence and the user's request.

## Research and contribution

- Scientific problem and intended audience.
- Closest relevant prior result and the remaining limitation; distinguish checked references from literature-search needs.
- Central contribution in one sentence.
- Strongest supported claim, with scope and conditions.
- Desired stronger claim, if different, and the evidence needed to earn it.

## Evidence inventory

| Finding or claim | Completed data/source | Controls and uncertainty | Alternative explanation | Supported wording | Unresolved gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep completed work, reanalysis, and proposed experiments distinct. Identify a missing input rather than filling it with invented values.

## Figure sequence

| Figure | Question | Data/panels | Contribution to the argument | Assumption or limit | Next unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Test whether every major conclusion has evidence, every main figure has a purpose, and the proposed sequence lets a reader understand each inference. The plan may differ from the final numbering while the story is being revised.

## Contribution assessment

Assess importance, originality, evidential strength, explanatory depth, scope/robustness, and clarity using qualitative, reasoned judgments. State the strongest case for broad interest and the strongest objection. Avoid predicting editorial outcomes or counting techniques as evidence of quality.

## Drafting plan

- Introduction: important problem -> specific gap -> study's contribution.
- Results: argument steps connected to the figure sequence.
- Discussion: integrated explanation, alternatives, scope, limitations, implications.
- Abstract/title: contribution and claim strength consistent with the evidence.

## Next actions

Separate essential validity work, useful strengthening work, and prose/figure improvements. For a proposed experiment, specify the competing predictions and the decision its result would change. Where data are unavailable, provide a conditional outline rather than a fictional results section.
