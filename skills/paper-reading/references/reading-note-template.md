# Reading note structure

Create one Markdown note per paper. Adapt the detail to what was actually discussed; empty sections need not be populated with speculation.

## Paper and access

- Title, authors, publication year, venue, DOI or stable identifier.
- Source location and material actually inspected: main text, figures, supplementary information, relevant prior papers.
- Date of the latest substantive update.

## Central argument

- Scientific problem and why it matters.
- Prior limitation or competing explanations.
- Authors' central claim.
- Strongest claim supported by our inspected evidence.
- Plain-language prerequisite definitions needed for this reader.

## Figure argument map

| Figure/panels | Question | What is actually shown | Inference and assumption | Why the argument needs it | Next question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Preserve important normalization, controls, units, uncertainty, and limits. Mark schematic panels as schematics rather than measured evidence.

## Claim and quality assessment

Use the evaluation framework for the main claims. Keep the top-tier case separate from direct evidence of editorial decisions. Include historical context and unchecked novelty comparisons.

## Questions, comments, and confusion

For each substantive item:

- Stable ID and date.
- User's question or comment, quoted briefly or faithfully paraphrased.
- Type: question, interpretation, preference, or workflow correction.
- Evidence-backed evaluation and relevant source anchors.
- Explanation attempted and what remains unclear.
- Status: `open`, `explained-awaiting-confirmation`, `revisit`, or `user-confirmed-understanding`.
- Next useful step. Distinguish missing data from a concept the user wants explained.

## Focus and lessons for writing

- Explicit user priorities and any tentative inferred needs, labeled separately.
- Scientific lessons specific to this paper.
- Reusable research/writing methods, their applicability, and the supporting example.
- Skill improvements made, their rationale, and any installation synchronization needed.
- Next question to revisit; do not impose an unsolicited exam or a reading schedule.

## Cross-paper learning profile

Maintain a separate project-local `reading-notes/learning-profile.md` with:

- Explicit goals and explanation preferences, with dates or source note IDs.
- Active paper identifier and links to notes when helpful.
- Open or recurring confusion, using the same evidence-based status rules.
- Demonstrated understanding only when supported by the user's confirmation or reasoning.
- Priorities for future reading and writing.

Preserve changes of mind. Update or supersede stale preferences rather than accumulating contradictory rules. Keep this personal record separate from shareable skill sources.
