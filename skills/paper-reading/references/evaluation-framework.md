# Evaluating a paper's scientific contribution

Use this when assessing a whole paper or deriving a reusable research/writing lesson. It is a diagnostic framework, not a journal-acceptance formula.

## Establish the claim before judging the paper

Write the problem, the previous limitation, the authors' proposed advance, and the strongest conclusion that the inspected evidence supports. State whether the prior-work comparison was independently checked. For an older paper, separate its historical contribution from current understanding.

For a mechanistic paper, identify the competing models and their differing predictions. For a methods paper, identify the task, relevant baselines, meaningful improvements, and failure regimes. For a descriptive or discovery paper, identify the observation, its reliability, and what becomes newly explainable or testable. Do not force every paper into a causal-mechanism format.

## Trace claim to evidence

For each consequential claim, record:

| Claim | Measured observation and source | Assumptions needed | Competing explanation and test | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use qualitative assessments such as `strongly supported`, `supported with limits`, `suggestive`, `unsupported`, or `not assessed`. Explain the assessment. Avoid a single numerical quality score by default; it conceals different weaknesses and suggests a precision the evidence does not provide.

Useful checks:

- Does the observable measure the claimed process, or also respond to transport, selection, normalization, instrument artifacts, or other processes?
- Are the predicted outcomes actually different under the competing explanations? A null result only constrains effects within the experiment's sensitivity, time scale, and tested conditions.
- Are controls, sample selection, comparisons, uncertainties, and model assumptions adequate for the size of the claim?
- Do different experiments supply independent constraints or repeat the same assumption?
- Does a fitted activation energy, effective parameter, or structural proxy have a unique microscopic interpretation?
- Does a relationship establish association, mechanism, or causality? What single change or additional control would make the inference more discriminating?

## Assess the contribution on separate dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence to seek | Useful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Importance | A consequential unresolved problem or capability | What changes for the field if the central claim is true? |
| Originality | Specific advance over the closest relevant work | What can this paper conclude or do that earlier work could not? |
| Evidential strength | Discriminating observations, controls, reliable analysis | Why should a skeptical expert accept this conclusion? |
| Explanatory depth | A justified mechanism, principle, or coherent account | Does it explain why, or mainly report a correlation? |
| Scope and robustness | Relevant systems, conditions, exceptions, and reproducibility | Where does the conclusion hold, and where is it untested? |
| Communication and fit | A clear argument whose figures serve its claims | Can the intended audience see the advance and its support? |

Use `strong`, `mixed`, `weak`, or `unknown` where useful, with source-backed reasons. The appropriate emphasis depends on the paper's type. Broad scope is not automatically superior to a narrow but decisive discovery.

## Explain a plausible top-tier case

Give a reasoned case, not a presumed editorial verdict:

- The important question or disputed issue addressed.
- The specific conceptual or methodological advance.
- The decisive evidence and the strongest remaining objection.
- The potential interest beyond the immediate experiment.
- The contribution's boundaries and what would weaken the case.

Distinguish scientific strength from polished presentation, timeliness, editorial fit, and prestige. More techniques, more figures, or higher device performance alone do not establish a stronger contribution. If direct comparisons have not been checked, qualify novelty judgments.

## Transfer reading into writing

For each useful lesson, record `source example -> general method -> conditions where it applies -> action in a new study`.

Examples of methods to evaluate, rather than universal prescriptions:

- Pair techniques when their observables resolve different ambiguities.
- Order results so each figure answers a question raised by the preceding evidence.
- After a model-discriminating result, test the strongest plausible alternative interpretation.
- Use an independent observable to constrain a shared mechanism.
- Present a correlation as a correlation when variables are not independently controlled.

End a substantial assessment with the best-supported advance and the most important inferential limit. Do not predict a journal decision or suggest wording can repair missing evidence.
